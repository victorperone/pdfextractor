## Short OCR Baseline

The `balanced` mode was run with the `baseline` policy, without modifying the
OCR code, on controls 1, 2, 13, 15–16, 19, 27, 42, 46, 49, 50, and 53.
Commands used `PYTHONPATH=src` and saved Markdown, log, and `/usr/bin/time`
statistics to `/tmp/ocr-stress-audit/`.

Observed results:

- page 1 and pages 13, 19, 46, 49, 50, and 53 preserved the native layer;
- page 2 recovered raster text with accents, currency, date, and percentage;
- pages 15–16 recovered the markers and common content of the continuous figure;
- page 27 recovered the raster table with cells and header;
- page 42 recovered raster text in landscape;
- batch 46–50 preserved QR/label, code/label, partial image, and the fully
  external figure without inventing OCR text for the invisible region.

Recorded wall times: p1 0.53 s; p2 23.01 s; p13–14 22.48 s;
p15–16 36.90 s; p19 0.27 s; p27 21.42 s; p42 22.94 s; p46–50 10.75 s;
p53 0.29 s. Logs showed no execution errors; environment warnings such as
missing `ccache` and temporary Matplotlib cache did not interrupt the
extractions.

## Limitations and Scope Decision

- The visual inspection was sampled, not a claim of full visual review of all
  60 pages.
- The OCR baseline was short and controlled; not all policies were run on all
  pages.
- Local Code128 validation was limited to generation and geometry because the
  environment lacks a barcode decoder.
- No changes were made to native reconstruction, text assembly, reading order,
  deduplication, ledger, tables, or production OCR.

The corpus and its infrastructure are ready for the next step. Production
changes should begin only after responsible approval of this report; after
that, the next step is to record each failure reproduced in the corpus and
apply one isolated OCR fix at a time, comparing against this baseline.
